🚪 3 doors. 1 car, 2 goats. You pick door 1. The host (who knows where the car is) opens door 3: a goat. Do you STICK or SWITCH? Comment your answer BEFORE reading on 👇

PARADOX #3: THE MONTY HALL PROBLEM

Most people say "two doors left, 50-50".
❌ Wrong. Switching wins 2 out of 3 times. It DOUBLES your chance.

📰 In 1990, Marilyn vos Savant wrote "yes, you should switch". Thousands of readers, including people with PhDs, wrote in to say she was wrong. She was right.

1️⃣ Your first pick: it had a 1/3 chance and that never changes. The other two doors together hold 2/3. The host removes a goat, and the whole 2/3 lands on the last door.
2️⃣ Every case: car behind 1 → switching loses. Car behind 2 → you win. Car behind 3 → you win. Switching wins 2 of 3.
3️⃣ 100 doors: you pick one, the host opens 98 goats. Stick with your random pick, or switch to the ONE door he avoided? Obviously switch.

💻 1,000 games on a computer: sticking won 333 cars, switching won 667.

🔑 The key is the host: he knows where the car is and never opens it. His choice isn't random, so it gives you information.
🧠 Real life: when new information arrives, update your odds. That's Bayesian thinking (doctors, spam filters, data scientists).
