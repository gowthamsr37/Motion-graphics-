You flip a coin 10 times and get 9 heads 🪙 Is it rigged, or did you just get lucky? The P-VALUE answers exactly that 👇

🎤 INTERVIEW ANSWER: "The p-value is the probability of getting results at least as extreme as yours, assuming the null hypothesis is true."
💬 In plain words: if nothing special were going on, how surprising would my data be?

😴 The null hypothesis = the boring explanation: nothing special is going on. The coin is fair. The drug does nothing. The button changes nothing.

1️⃣ Example: 9 heads. If the coin were fair, 9 or more heads happens only about 1% of the time → p = 0.01. Very surprising, so we doubt the coin.
2️⃣ Example: 6 heads. A fair coin gets 6 or more heads 38% of the time → p = 0.38. Not surprising at all.
3️⃣ Example: a new drug lowers blood pressure more than a placebo, p = 0.02. If the drug did nothing, a gap this big would appear only 2% of the time → significant.

📏 The rule:
• Small p → surprising → evidence against the null
• Big p → not surprising → no evidence
• Below 0.05 → "statistically significant"

⚠️ 2 interview traps:
1. p = 0.02 is NOT a 2% chance the null is true. It's the chance of the data, if the null were true.
2. A small p-value doesn't mean a big effect. It only says the effect is probably not zero.
