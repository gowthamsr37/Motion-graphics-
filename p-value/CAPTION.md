Research papers love to say it: p < 0.05 📄 But what does a p-value actually MEAN? Let's make it simple.

📖 The definition, in simple words: the p-value is the chance of getting results AT LEAST AS EXTREME as yours, IF NOTHING SPECIAL were going on.
A small p-value means: this would be really surprising by luck alone.

🪙 Back to the coin that landed heads 9 times out of 10. If the coin were fair, 9 or 10 heads would happen just 1% of the time. So p = 0.01.

⚖️ Compare 6 heads out of 10: 6 or more heads happens 38% of the time, so p = 0.38.
• Small p → hard to explain by luck
• Big p → easily luck

📏 By convention, p below 0.05 is called "statistically significant". But that line is a habit, not a law of nature: 0.049 and 0.051 are almost the same evidence.

⚠️ The most common mistake: p = 0.01 does NOT mean a 1% chance that the null is true. It's the chance of the DATA if the null were true, not the chance of the null given the data.

🔍 And a tiny p doesn't mean a big effect. With 10 million users per group, 10.00% vs 10.05% gives p < 0.001, for a difference of just 0.05 points. Always check the effect size.
