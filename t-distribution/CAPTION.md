In 1908, a brewer at Guinness 🍺 had a problem: he could only test a few batches at a time. With so little data, the normal curve made him too confident. His fix: the t-DISTRIBUTION (published under the pen name "Student").

📖 The definition, in simple words:
1️⃣ It looks like the normal bell curve
2️⃣ but with FATTER TAILS
3️⃣ used for SMALL SAMPLES, when we guess the spread from the data itself
The smaller the sample, the fatter the tails.

🤔 Why fatter tails? With only a few values, our guess of the spread is shaky: five values can look tightly packed one time and widely spread the next. Fat tails leave room for that extra uncertainty.

🔢 Its shape depends on one number, the degrees of freedom = sample size − 1. Five values → 4 degrees of freedom.

📊 df = 4: fat tails. df = 10: slimmer. By about 30: almost exactly normal.

🎯 Why it matters: the middle 95% of the normal curve spans about ±2 (1.96) steps. With only 5 values, the t-curve needs about ±2.8 steps. Small samples give wider, more honest ranges.
