Rain cuts the chase from 50 overs to 25. So the target is halved, right? ❌

Team 1 makes 250. Run-rate maths says Team 2 need 126 off 25 overs. Under Duckworth–Lewis–Stern, it's around 170. 🌧️

Why? A batting team has two resources: overs left AND wickets in hand. 25 overs with all 10 wickets is about two-thirds of a full innings, not half, because those wickets let you attack.

The idea goes back to 1992, when an old rain rule left South Africa needing 21 runs off 1 ball in a World Cup semi-final. Statisticians Frank Duckworth and Tony Lewis built a fairer system, first used in 1997. Professor Steven Stern updated it in 2014, and that's the DLS we use today.

Overs + wickets = resources. Save this for the next rain delay ☔
