PARADOX #4: THE FALSE POSITIVE PARADOX

Most people say 99%. The real answer: about 9%. More than 9 out of 10 positives are false alarms.

👥 Picture 10,000 people:
🔴 1 in 1,000 is sick → 10 people. The test catches all 10 (true positives).
🟠 9,990 are healthy. The test is wrong for 1% of them → about 100 people told they're sick (false positives).
➗ All positives ≈ 110. Really sick: 10. 10 ÷ 110 ≈ 9%.

🧠 Why: the base rate. The disease is so rare that the healthy crowd is huge, and a tiny error rate on a huge crowd beats the number of real cases. Ignoring how rare something is = BASE RATE NEGLECT.

🩺 That's why doctors retest: after two positive tests, the chance you're really sick jumps to about 90%.

🌍 It's everywhere: bank fraud alerts when it was just you shopping, airport scanners beeping at innocent bags, real emails landing in spam. When the target is rare, most alarms are false.
