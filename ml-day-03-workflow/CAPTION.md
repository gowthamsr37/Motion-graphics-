Everyone thinks machine learning = picking the perfect algorithm 🤖 In real jobs, training the model is ~10% of the work. Here's the other 90% 👇

🗺️ DAY 3 / 100: THE MACHINE LEARNING WORKFLOW
One project, start to finish: predicting flat prices in Pune 🏠

1️⃣ DEFINE: predict the price (₹ lakh), a number, so regression. Success = off by under ₹5 lakh on average.
2️⃣ COLLECT: past sales (area, bedrooms, location, age, price). 10,000 flats.
3️⃣ CLEAN (the biggest step): missing prices, duplicate rows, typos like 12,000 sq ft instead of 1,200. Garbage in, garbage out.
4️⃣ EXPLORE: bigger flats cost more, near IT parks cost more, older buildings a little less.
5️⃣ SPLIT (beginners skip this!): hide 20% as a test set, train on 80%. If you've seen the exam paper, the score proves nothing.
6️⃣ TRAIN: guess → error → adjust, again and again (like Day 1).
7️⃣ EVALUATE + DEPLOY: on the 2,000 hidden flats, off by ₹4 lakh vs a ₹5 lakh target. Passed! Put it in an app, keep watching it, retrain as prices change.

## Pinned comment

✅ Answer: B! Testing on the training data is like grading a student on the exact questions they practised. The model may have just memorised them (overfitting). Always test on data the model has never seen: that's why we split 80/20. Did you get it? 👇
