A news poll says 52% of voters support a new metro line, ± 3% 📰 What does that "± 3" actually mean? It's a CONFIDENCE INTERVAL. Let's make it simple 👇

🤔 Why a range? Pollsters can't ask everyone, so they ask a sample (say 1,000 people). Another 1,000 might give 51%, or 54%. One number alone can mislead.

🎣 Think of fishing: a single number is like a spear, so you'll probably miss. A range is like a net, far more likely to catch the fish. A confidence interval is that net.

🎤 INTERVIEW ANSWER: "A confidence interval is a range of values, calculated from a sample, that is likely to contain the true population value. The confidence level (like 95%) tells how reliable that method is."

1️⃣ Example: 52% ± 3% → 49% to 55%. The true support is likely in there.
2️⃣ Example: 100 delivery orders, average 32 min → 95% CI: 30 to 34 minutes.

🧮 The recipe: estimate ± margin of error
• Margin ≈ 2 standard errors (1.96 for 95%)
• Standard error = spread ÷ √(sample size) = 10 ÷ √100 = 1
• 32 ± 2 → 30 to 34

⚠️ The part most people get wrong: 95% does NOT mean a 95% chance this one range is right. It means: repeat the survey 100 times, and about 95 of the ranges will catch the true value.

📏 More data → narrower (4× the sample halves the margin). More confidence → wider (99% needs a bigger net).
