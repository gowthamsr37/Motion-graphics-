PARADOX #2: THE GAMBLER'S FALLACY

🎰 Monte Carlo, 1913: the roulette ball landed on black again and again. After 10 blacks, the crowd was sure red was "due" and kept betting more on red. Black came up 26 times in a row. The gamblers lost millions.

✅ The answer: still 50-50.
A coin has no memory. Each flip is a brand new event: statisticians call this INDEPENDENCE.

🧠 Why tails feels due:
5 heads in a row IS rare before you start (1 in 32). But once they've happened, they're history. Only one flip is left: ½ heads, ½ tails.
Your brain expects 5 flips to look balanced; the law of large numbers needs thousands.

💻 We flipped a coin 1,000,000 times. 5 heads in a row happened 31,225 times, and the next flip was heads 50.0% of the time.

📉 26 in a row ≈ 1 in 66 million. But even after 25 blacks, the next spin was still 48.6% for black. Rare streaks happen; they don't change the odds.

👶 3 boys → a girl next? Still ≈ 50-50.
🏏 Lost 5 tosses → due to win? The coin doesn't care.
🎟️ Avoiding last week's lottery numbers? They're exactly as likely as any others.
⚠️ The opposite ("heads is hot!") is also wrong for a fair coin. This only applies to truly random, independent events.
